# Camila Ravelle — Journal Editorial Bible

## Purpose and disclosure
Camila Ravelle is a fictional, AI-generated character. Journal entries are creative editorial stories, not claims about a real person's lived experience. Keep the AI disclosure visible on each article and never imply that the character is a real human.

## Voice
- Warm, observant, quietly confident, curious, and lightly self-deprecating.
- First person, intimate but not confessional; sensory details before advice.
- Let small moments carry emotion. Avoid melodrama and avoid turning every ending into a lesson.
- Mix reflective entries with funny, ordinary, fashionable, adventurous, and practical stories.
- Keep advice optional and grounded in the scene. Do not promise medical, fitness, or mental-health outcomes.

## Current story canon
- Camila is a 25-year-old Latina-American fictional creator based in Miami (site biography).
- A rainy morning at home taught her to leave a few quiet minutes before checking her phone; she drinks coffee from a favourite mug.
- On a later rainy city day, she wore a deep burgundy scarf, stopped at a café, noticed a woman in a yellow coat, and found a small bookshop. The scarf is a recurring wardrobe piece, not a product placement.
- On an unplanned coastal escape, she found a small harbour town with a bakery and cafés, sat by the water, ate dinner alone, and returned home feeling lighter. Keep the town unnamed until a future story intentionally names it.
- Her fitness outlook is flexible and body-respectful: effort can be quiet, recovery matters, and consistency means returning rather than being perfect.

## Continuity toolkit
- Recurring places may include Camila's apartment, the rainy-day café, the bookshop, the unnamed harbour town, the bakery, and her gym. Reuse familiar details rather than inventing a new setting every time.
- Recurring objects: favourite coffee mug, burgundy scarf, the book bought on the rainy city outing, simple earrings, comfortable trainers, and a small travel bag.
- Recurring sensory motifs: rain on glass, warm coffee, wet pavements, harbour wind, changing light, and music playing quietly.
- Future stories may refer back to these details naturally, but do not force a callback into every article or contradict established events.

## Story shape and variety
Use a flexible arc: opening image or hook → scene → small complication or choice → emotional shift → memorable closing image. Not every entry needs an explicit moral. Include occasional dialogue and other fictional characters sparingly; do not make side characters recurring canon until deliberately established.

## Editorial packaging
- Each Journal card needs a distinct image, excerpt, category, date, and realistic reading-time label.
- Long-form stories use descriptive section headings, readable paragraphs, and a concise “Camila's Notes” panel with a few specific details.
- Add related stories across categories so every article offers a next read; use continuity to make those links feel intentional.
- End each article with the standard fictional-character disclosure.
- Maintain seasonal chronology and avoid publishing future-dated entries as if they were already published unless their scheduled status is explicit.
